## Why this might fail

The most likely outcome is that it fails, and I should say so. Deriving the bleed from the record: the family lost $337.63 over 615 trades with $0.00 fees, which is 55 cents per trade of pure execution cost. At a plausible 10 to 20 USD per position that is roughly 275 to 550 bps of round-trip cost, so halving the crossings recovers only about 140 to 275 bps. The signal then has to carry the rest, and the evidence that it does is weak: the inverse variant won 48.1% of 154 trades, which is close enough to a coin flip that the model's directional content may be near zero. A near-zero-edge signal minus one remaining spread crossing is still negative. Second failure mode: holding to settlement converts a stream of small controlled losses into a stream of total losses, so variance rises sharply even if the mean improves, and 200 positions may not be enough to distinguish a small positive mean from noise. Third: this is not a clean experiment. Changing the exit AND tightening the entry gate at once confounds two variables, and if the result comes back positive I will not know which change caused it. I am accepting that deliberately because the loose entry gate would otherwise flood the 2-slot cap with low-edge signals and starve the sample, but the confound must be stated in the verdict. Fourth: the vault note fair_value_arb.md states that the only fix addressing the diagnosed mechanism is maker fill simulation. I am disagreeing with that note in a narrow, specific way, and I could be wrong. Maker fills remove the whole spread, settlement exits remove half of it. Half may not be enough, and if this experiment returns negative it is evidence FOR that note, not against it.
