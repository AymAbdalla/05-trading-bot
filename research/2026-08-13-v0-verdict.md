# The v0 Verdict: 33 of 35 Strategies Lose Exactly the Transaction Cost

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Status:** the SPEC's T7 "moment of truth", finally answerable
**Data:** 218,295 result rows, 180 tickers, 4 timeframes, 35 strategies,
9 exit configs, on the fixed harness with adjusted data

## Two findings, and the second one is the answer

### Finding 1: most strategies could never be judged per-ticker

The silent assertions caught it: **13 of 35 strategies NEVER once reached the
20-trade verdict floor in 212,058 runs.** The SPEC's own core patterns are
among them:

| Pattern | Runs reaching 20 trades | Max trades ever |
|---|---|---|
| morning_star | 0 of 6,237 | 6 |
| piercing_line | 0 of 6,237 | 8 |
| bullish_engulfing | 17 of 6,237 (0.27%) | 27 |
| hammer | 18 of 6,237 (0.29%) | 25 |

Every one of those runs was recorded FAIL. That reads as "the pattern does not
work" when the truth is "this test could not answer the question." A 251-bar
test window cannot produce 20 instances of a pattern that fires on ~1% of
bars. The arithmetic made it impossible before any market behavior was
involved.

**Fix: pool across tickers.** A candlestick pattern's edge is a property of
the pattern, not of AAPL specifically. Pooling turns 1.1 trades per run into
thousands. `backtest/pooled_analysis.py`. After pooling, 33 of 35 strategies
became judgeable (only rising_three_methods and rsi_extreme remain too rare,
at 45 pooled trades each - both are near-unsatisfiable by construction, see
below).

### Finding 2: pooled, every strategy loses almost exactly the trading cost

| Strategy | Pooled trades | Win rate | **PnL per trade** |
|---|---|---|---|
| grid_1.0atr | 259,362 | 19.7% | **-$0.297** |
| fair_value_gap | 165,110 | 13.9% | **-$0.297** |
| stoch_rsi_oversold | 121,575 | 17.4% | **-$0.290** |
| dca_7 | 93,890 | 24.7% | **-$0.272** |
| breakout_20 | 65,622 | 23.6% | **-$0.314** |
| bullish_engulfing | 7,140 | 21.7% | **-$0.256** |
| hammer | 6,839 | 18.3% | **-$0.300** |
| morning_star | 1,699 | 21.7% | **-$0.398** |
| piercing_line | 1,378 | 17.8% | **-$0.270** |

Round-trip cost on a $100 position: 0.20% fees + 0.10% slippage = **$0.30.**

Every strategy clusters at -$0.25 to -$0.35 per trade. That is not a
coincidence and it is not a spread of outcomes. **It means gross edge is
indistinguishable from zero across the entire v0 library.** These strategies
are not bad at predicting direction; they are not predicting anything. Net
result = -(transaction cost), which is what a coin flip pays.

This is the same signature the inversion study produced independently
(-$0.31 per exit against a $0.30 cost). Two unrelated analyses, same number,
same conclusion.

Two exceptions worth noting, neither of them good news:
- **mat_hold: -$0.016/trade on 396 trades.** Nearly break-even gross, but 31
  tickers and 34% win rate; most likely small-sample noise, and it is still
  negative.
- **C5: -$0.709/trade on 4,204 trades.** Materially WORSE than costs, meaning
  genuinely negative gross edge. Per SPEC 5.6 it is an inversion candidate,
  though the inversion study already showed fades do not survive costs.

## What this does and does not prove

**Proves:** on this universe (180 large/mid-cap US equities, ETFs, 8 futures,
3 crypto pairs), at these timeframes (5m/15m/1h/1d), with these exit families
(fixed R, trailing ATR, time-based), the v0 strategy library has no
measurable edge. The SPEC's expectation that v0 patterns would mostly fail is
confirmed, and now it is confirmed on a harness that has passed its own
validation rather than assumed.

**Does not prove:** that candlestick patterns never work, that these
strategies fail on other instruments/timeframes/exits, or that the confirmed
finding survives regime changes. The v2 ticker-fingerprinting idea (route
strategies by instrument character) remains untested and is the most
promising unexplored direction, precisely because pooling assumes homogeneity
that fingerprinting would reject.

## Two strategies are structurally unsatisfiable, not merely bad

- **rsi_extreme** requires price ABOVE the 50-EMA and RSI BELOW 35
  simultaneously. Those conditions nearly contradict: RSI under 35 usually
  means price has already fallen below the 50-EMA. 45 trades in 6,237 runs.
- **rising_three_methods** demands a rare 5-candle geometry; 45 trades total.

These are the same class of defect as the tasuki gaps (unsatisfiable
comparisons) and FairValueGap (empty loop), except the conditions are
individually valid and only jointly near-impossible. Worth either relaxing
deliberately or retiring.

## Consequences for the build

1. **Per-ticker verdicts are the wrong unit for rare patterns.** The graveyard
   should carry pooled verdicts alongside per-ticker rows, and the SPEC 5.3
   acceptance bar (150 trades) should be applied to the POOLED count.
2. **The gate needs a "not enough data to judge" verdict distinct from FAIL,**
   applied per-strategy, not just for window-size failures (D-109 extended).
3. **Forge must not be handed this graveyard as "35 failed strategies."** The
   honest statement is: 33 measured, all at zero gross edge; 2 unmeasurable.
   The difference matters enormously for what Forge tries next.
4. **The next real question is not "which pattern works" but "does any
   instrument-strategy pairing have edge at all"** - which is the v2
   fingerprinting thesis, and it now has a strong prior that generic pattern
   trading does not.

# Addendum: Pooled by ASSET CLASS (Aym's follow-up)

**Question:** the strategies that cannot reach 20 trades on any single ticker
are generic patterns, not ticker-specific ones. How do they behave across
asset classes?

**Answer: pooling by class rescues them, and the verdict is unchanged.**
`backtest/asset_class_analysis.py`.

## Coverage rescued

**105 of 128 strategy-class cells became judgeable** (>=150 pooled trades),
where per-ticker almost none were. Only the genuinely rare patterns
(rsi_extreme, rising_three_methods, bullish_abandoned_baby) stay below the
bar even pooled across a whole class.

## Every judgeable cell is still negative

Round-trip cost is -$0.30/trade. The judgeable cells run from -0.036 to
-1.321, and the great majority sit within a few cents of -0.30. The
cross-class SPREAD is small for almost every strategy (0.06-0.36), which is
itself the finding: **these strategies are not asset-class-specific, they are
uniformly at zero.** That is evidence against the simple form of the
fingerprinting thesis. Routing a strategy to a better asset class does not
help when it has no edge in any class.

## The best cell in the entire study is one asset in a costume

`bullish_harami` on CRYPTO reported **-0.036/trade over 162 trades** - by far
the closest thing to break-even anywhere in the graveyard, and exactly the
kind of number that gets promoted.

Broken down by underlying:

| Asset | Trades | PnL | Per trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOL | 54 | +$39.19 | **+0.726** |
| ETH | 45 | -$21.84 | -0.485 |
| BTC | 63 | -$23.19 | -0.368 |

**Without SOL it is -0.417/trade, WORSE than the cost floor.** And within SOL,
a single 9-trade cell (SOL/USDT 1h) produced +2.534/trade. The headline was
carried by nine trades.

Only 2 of 10 ticker-timeframe cells in that group were profitable.

## New automatic guard

The analysis now runs a **leave-one-asset-out** check on every cell and flags
any result that moves more than 0.15/trade when its top underlying is
removed. It groups by UNDERLYING, so BTC/USDT and BTC_USD count as one asset
rather than two independent confirmations.

Three cells are flagged as one-asset results:
- bullish_harami CRYPTO: -0.036 -> -0.417 without SOL
- bullish_engulfing FUTURES: -0.165 -> -0.359 without CL
- C5 ETF: -1.321 -> -1.563 without LABU

Nobody should have to notice this by hand again.

## What this changes

1. Pooled-by-class is now the correct default reading for any rare pattern,
   and the SPEC 5.3 150-trade bar should be applied at that level.
2. The fingerprinting thesis is NOT supported in its simple form. Strategies
   do not have hidden edge in a particular asset class; they have no edge
   anywhere. If fingerprinting is to work it must condition on something
   finer than asset class (volatility regime, spread, gap propensity).
3. At 105 comparisons, chance alone yields roughly a 3.1 sigma best result.
   The top cell must clear that bar before it means anything, and this one
   does not survive its own leave-one-out test.
