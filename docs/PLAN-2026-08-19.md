**The fade direction is not established, and D-326 was ruled on evidence that

The edge analysis reported that mirroring the 345 settled positions flips

Splitting that same number by **how the fill happened**:

| TAKER settled (**executable**) | 169 | 1,452 | 0.222 | 0.183 | +55.73 | **+51.15** | **1.52** |

`MAKER-fill` = `PM_grid_hedge`, `PM_box_builder`, `PM_maker_rebate_quote_ladder`,
`PM_streak_snapper` — the four strategies whose positions are opened by
`PolymarketPaperAdapter.observe_resting_orders`, not `simulate_taker_buy`.

**80% of the fade evidence comes from maker fills, and a maker fill cannot be
mirrored.** Two independent reasons, one of them mechanical:

1. **The counterfactual does not exist.** Those fills happened because someone
   crossed our resting bid. "Take the other side" means being the party that
   lifts our own offer at a price the market has already left. There is no
   trade there.

2. **The loss is produced by the fill rule itself, not by the market.**
   `paper_adapter.py:1088 _through_and_touch`: for a resting BUY at `L`,
   `through` is the size offered **strictly below `L`** in the ask book. So a
   resting bid fills **only in the states of the world where the best ask has
   already fallen through `L`**, and `_fill_resting_buy` (line 1461) then books
   every share **at `L`**, the pre-move price. That is adverse selection by
   construction: there is no path in this model where a resting bid fills on a
   benign touch. `grid_hedge` paying 0.213 and realising 0.147 is the fill rule
   restating itself, not a mispricing.

   To be fair to the simulator: this is the **conservative** choice and the
   docstring says so ("Change `<` to `<=` here and both maker strategies become
   profitable on paper for no reason at all"). It is good modelling. It is just
   not a market measurement, and its mirror is not a strategy.

**What is left after the decomposition is `t = 1.52`.** Not significant. And of
that +$51.15, roughly half is 29 deep-longshot trades (`dip_arb` n=19 at ask
0.045, `fair_value_arb` n=5 at 0.043, `hft` n=5 at 0.025) where **every single
one settled at zero**. Mirroring those means selling 4-cent lottery tickets:
risking 0.96 to make 0.04, needing a 96% hit rate, on 29 observations that all
won. That is the exact shape that prints for months and then removes the
account.

## 1. The fact that should reorganise the whole program

`PM_temporal_arbitrage`, 83 positions, **zero censoring** (no stop, no target,
100% held to settlement, every position a distinct market):

```
shares 415   paid 0.1813   realised 0.1807   edge -0.0006
```

**The market price is calibrated to within six hundredths of one percentage
point.** This is the cleanest measurement in the database: no fill model, no
stop, no selection by outcome, no pooling across mechanisms.

It says the thing nobody has said out loud yet: **there is no large directional
mispricing to find in these markets.** Every strategy in the registry —
book-imbalance, longshot-fade, weather-bracket, settlement-exit, status-quo,
smart-money-callers, and all six fair_value variants — is a *forecasting*
strategy, i.e. a bet that we can beat that price. On the only unbiased sample
we own, that price cannot be beaten by a measurable amount.

That does not mean nothing works. It means **forecasting is the wrong family**,
and the answer to Q5 is yes, forced by measurement rather than chosen by taste.

Caveat, stated plainly: n=83, one strategy, two days, one market family (BTC
15m up/down). It bounds the edge loosely, not tightly. But it is the only
number in the book that is not contaminated by a stop, a fill model, or a
selection rule, and it points the same way as the price-bucket table (below),
where the win rate tracks the market price and not the model at every level.

**The strongest counter-argument to `PM_streak_snapper` is not its sample size.
It is that `streak_snapper` is a MAKER strategy.** Its entire record (+$4.52 on

### Q2 — the 034 gate

**Raven's worry targets the wrong gate.** The `ask <= 0.60` cap is close to
non-binding: it removes only **13.2%** of the eligible population (12,244 ->
10,630 signals) and in the live record it refused only **5 of 1,131** signals
(`settlement_entry_ask_above_cap`). It is not what is making 034 rare.

**What is making 034 rare is `max_trades_this_window`: 643 of 1,131 skips
(57%).** Keep the 0.60 cap — it costs almost nothing and it bounds the tail —
and relax the throttle. That is what the mirror-fade directive's Task 2 already
says, and it is right.

**Yes to the frequency kill, with a number:** if 034 has not reached **60
settled entries within 14 days** of the throttle being relaxed, it is not a
measurable instrument and it retires. Paired with the outcome kill Raven
already recorded: dead if realised settlement frequency over the first 60
entries is below 0.30 against mean entry ask 0.33.

**One correction to how 034 gets described.** Do not raise its edge threshold
to make it fire. The quartile tables show results get *worse* as claimed edge
rises (`hft`: 45.7% -> 20.2% win rate from Q1 to Q4). Tightening the edge gate
selects harder *for* the model error. 034's value is the calibration number,
whatever its sign — it is an instrument, not a profit strategy, and its
expected result is roughly 25% realised against a 33% requirement.

### Q3 — the ONE measurement we're missing

**Not the half-spread.** Execution is ~9% of the loss, and I measured the
complement cost directly tonight from `market_tape` (7,312 same-timestamp
complement candidates): **median overround 0.0020/share, mean 0.00316, p90
0.0060.** Buying the other side costs about a third of a cent per share more
than `1 - entry`. That is now known, and it is small enough that it does not
change any conclusion. Half-spread is answered.

**The ONE measurement is the complement token's own ask at entry
(`counter_ask`), stamped on every signal — together with the complement token's
identity.**

Three reasons, in order of force:

1. **It is not derivable from anything we store.** `features_json` has
   `best_ask` and no `best_bid` at all (58 keys, checked). `market_tape.market_id`
   is a token id; `positions.pair` is a market slug (`btc-updown-15m-1787025600`).
   **There is no complement mapping anywhere in the database.** I tried to
   recover one by matching same-timestamp mids summing to 1.0, and it
   over-matches badly: **61.7% of token-timestamps get more than one candidate
   partner** (1,636 of 2,652). So my own overround number above is indicative,
   not exact, and I am labelling it that way.

2. **It is a prerequisite for the strategy Raven just approved.** The
   mirror-fade probe must buy the complement at its own ask. It cannot price
   its own entry from recorded data, and the +$282 headline silently assumed
   the complement costs exactly `1 - entry`.

3. **It blocks a whole strategy family from even being tested** — see Q4. My
   attempt to check for `yes_ask + no_ask < 1` (free money) came back at 7.85%
   of pairs, and I am **reporting that as NOT_TESTED, not as a finding**,
   because the pairing that produced it is the same 61.7%-ambiguous heuristic
   and the hits are concentrated in exactly the duplicated far-from-0.5 rows
   where false pairs live. Convention 11. This is the single highest-value
   unanswered question in the project and we currently cannot ask it.

**Mandatory companion, one column:** `positions.fill_was_maker` (boolean).
Right now you cannot tell from the `positions` table whether a fill came from
the taker path or the maker simulator — I had to infer it from strategy names
and then read the adapter to confirm. **That missing boolean is what let 80%
non-executable evidence get pooled with 20% executable evidence and produce
D-326.** One column would have prevented tonight's error. Convention 30's
sibling: the strategy name is not the system of record for fill provenance.

### Q4 — the Forge brief, sharpened

**Every existing proposal is a forecasting strategy.** Book-imbalance,
longshot-fade, weather-bracket, settlement-exit, status-quo, smart-money-callers
— all six are bets that we predict the outcome better than the price does.
Section 1 says the price is calibrated to 0.0006 on the only clean sample we
have. **The missing family is the one whose PnL does not require predicting the
outcome at all.**

**Brief for Forge: propose structural, forecast-free strategies. Three named
directions, in priority order.**

1. **Complement no-arbitrage (highest value, currently unmeasurable).** Buy YES
   and NO together whenever `yes_ask + no_ask < 1`; the pair settles at exactly
   $1 regardless of outcome. Zero forecast, zero directional risk, bounded by
   fill risk on the second leg only. **Blocked on Q3**: we cannot identify
   complements from stored data. First deliverable is therefore the
   instrumentation, not the strategy. Kill condition once instrumented:
   dead if fewer than 20 genuine `yes_ask + no_ask <= 0.995` windows are
   observed in 7 days of tape.

2. **Cross-market monotonicity.** Within a family of related markets (BTC above
   strike K for several K, or bracketed weather ranges), prices must be
   monotone in strike and the brackets must sum to 1. Violations are
   forecast-free. This inherits `033`'s bracket machinery and needs the same
   complement/family mapping as (1).

3. **Resolution-mechanics edge.** Not "what will happen" but "what does the
   resolution source say and when." The settlement rule, its data source, and
   its timing are public and mechanical. This is the only forecasting-adjacent
   direction I would keep, because the thing being predicted is a *rule*, not a
   price.

**Explicitly tell Forge what NOT to propose:** anything that needs the
fair_value model, anything whose edge is a maker fill (the fill model makes
those uninterpretable until `fill_was_maker` exists), and anything justified by
a time-of-day window — no window survives permutation (best bucket p = 0.342
over 2,000 shuffles).

### Q5 — the honest question

**Yes, and it is forced rather than chosen.** Not because low-yield-consistent
is a nicer style, but because section 1 measured the price as unbiased. If
there is no large mispricing, then there is no high-edge-low-frequency trade to
find, and anything that makes money here makes it in fractions of a cent per
share on high count, out of *structure* — overround, queue position, resolution
mechanics — not out of forecasting.

**One caution on the reference wallet.** $412K on $2M turnover at ~1.8% ROI is
a real result, but it is not a regime we are in: we have $1,000 of paper equity
and moved 4,916 shares in roughly two days. Copying the *shape* (many small
structural trades) is right. Reading its ROI as our target is not — at our size
the per-trade structural edge has to clear a floor the reference wallet does
not face.

So: **env B should skew forecast-free and high-count.** That is a direction for
the *next* whitelist, not a reason to rebuild tonight's — nothing forecast-free
exists in the registry yet, which is exactly what Q4's brief is for.

## 5. What to tell Aym in the morning

We spent the night finding out that the thing we thought we'd found isn't
there. Yesterday's analysis correctly killed the "spreads are eating us" story:
execution is about 9% of the -$870, and the real problem is that the model buys
contracts worth less than it pays. That much holds. But it also reported that
flipping every settled trade would have turned -$294 into +$282, and Raven
approved building a strategy on that. Tonight I split that number by how the
trade was actually filled, and 80% of it comes from four strategies whose fills
are produced by our own maker simulator — a resting bid that, by the rule's own
design, can only fill after the price has already moved against it. You can't
take the other side of that; the trade never existed. What's left of the fade
once you keep only the trades we could really have made is +$51 at t = 1.52,
which is not a signal, and half of *that* is 29 four-cent lottery tickets that
all happened to expire worthless. So I've asked to hold the mirror-fade build
rather than ship it. Two nights of work now say the same uncomfortable thing
from different directions: on the one clean measurement we have — 83
uncensored trades where we paid 0.1813 and the outcomes came in at 0.1807 — the
market's price is right and we are not beating it. Every strategy in the
registry is a bet that we can forecast better than that price. That's the
problem, not the spread and not the concurrency cap. The honest next move is to
stop building forecasters and go after the structural trades where you don't
have to be right about the outcome at all — and the first blocker there is
embarrassingly basic: we don't record which token is the opposite side of which
market, so we literally cannot check whether both sides ever trade for less
than a dollar together. That's tonight's build, and it's a measurement, not a
strategy. Nothing is live, nothing is at risk, and the account is paper.
